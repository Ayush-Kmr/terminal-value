# Visual identity

The goal: every frame should be recognisable as this channel at a glance, while
no two episodes look like the same template. Consistent *system*, varied *output*.

## Core look

Dark, archival, slightly clinical. Think annual-report-meets-crime-board.
Near-black background (#0E1116), bone-white type, exactly one accent colour
(#E8B931 amber) that only ever marks the thing the viewer should look at.
Red (#D1485A) is reserved exclusively for decline — losses, layoffs, the fall.

Film grain at 3.5% over everything. This is the trick that makes AI stills,
archival photos and stock footage sit in the same world instead of looking
like a scrapbook.

## Shot types

| Type | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `stat_reveal` | A number that should land | One number, huge, held 3s in silence before narration resumes |
| `chart` | Revenue, share price, store count, headcount | Animated draw-on. Always labelled, always sourced on screen |
| `timeline` | Compressing years | Horizontal, amber marker moving left to right |
| `quote_card` | A real quote from a real filing or interview | Must carry attribution + date on screen. Never invented |
| `photo` | Archival / stock | Slow Ken Burns, max 6% zoom. Faster than that looks cheap |
| `ai_still` | Mood, era, interior, abstract concept | Never real people, never logos, never readable text |
| `document` | A filing, memo or court exhibit | Highlight the one line that matters in amber |

## Hard rules

- **No logos generated by AI.** Ever. Use the real mark under fair-use commentary
  or don't show it. AI-generated logos look wrong and viewers notice instantly.
- **No AI-generated faces of real people.** This is both a policy risk and a
  credibility risk. Use real archival photographs or use nothing.
- **No readable text in AI images.** Models still get it wrong and it is the
  single clearest "this is AI" signal to a viewer.
- **Charts must be real data with a source caption.** A fabricated chart is the
  fastest way to lose a business audience permanently.
- **Minimum 4 seconds per shot.** Rapid cutting over narration reads as slop.

## Thumbnail system

Three fixed layouts, rotated so the channel page has rhythm without chaos:

1. **The number** — one enormous figure (-94%), company mark small, face absent.
2. **The object** — single product shot on black, dramatically lit, amber rim.
3. **The person** — archival photo of the key executive, desaturated, amber
   accent line. Expression neutral, never the open-mouthed shock face.

Maximum four words of text. Never the same four words as the title — the
thumbnail and title should combine into one idea, not repeat each other.
