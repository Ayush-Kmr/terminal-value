# Packaging playbook

Research decides whether a video is good. Packaging decides whether anyone finds
out. On a channel this size, packaging is worth more of your attention than
production quality, and it's the thing you can actually iterate on.

## The only three numbers that matter early

1. **Click-through rate** — thumbnail + title. Healthy is 4–8%. Under 3% means
   packaging, full stop, regardless of how good the film is.
2. **Average view percentage** — the cold open and the first third. Under 30%
   means your opening is wrong, not your topic.
3. **Impressions** — if this is flat, YouTube isn't showing the video to anyone,
   which usually means it failed its first small test audience on CTR or
   retention. Fix those; impressions follow.

View count is downstream of all three and tells you nothing actionable.

## Titles

The pipeline generates eight, lints them, and ranks them. The lint rules:

- Under 60 characters, or it truncates on mobile search
- Company name present — that's the actual search term
- **"The Rise and Fall of X" is banned.** It is the most saturated format in this
  niche and it promises the viewer nothing they can't already guess.
- No ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks, no "shocking", "exposed", "insane"
- A real number from the research earns a bonus

The test for a good title here: **does it state a claim that could be wrong?**
"What Happened to Jawbone" is a topic. "Jawbone Raised $930M and Shipped Nothing"
is a claim. Claims get clicked; topics get scrolled past.

## Thumbnails

Three layouts, rotated, so the channel page has rhythm without chaos:

1. **The number** — one enormous figure, nothing else. Highest CTR of the three
   in this niche by a wide margin. Use it for anything with a dramatic decline.
2. **The object** — single product on black, dramatic light, amber rim.
3. **The person** — archival photo of the key executive, desaturated, amber
   accent line. Neutral expression. Never the open-mouthed shock face; it signals
   a different, lower-trust kind of channel and this audience is allergic to it.

Rules: maximum four words. Readable at 120px wide — check it by shrinking it,
not by looking at it full size. Never repeat the title's words — title and
thumbnail should combine into one idea, not say the same thing twice.

## The first 30 seconds

This is where most of your retention is won or lost, which is why the pipeline
generates five cold opens and scores them rather than writing one.

- No channel name, no logo sting, no "welcome back"
- No rhetorical question — automatic 25-point penalty in the scorer
- A specific number or a specific date in the first sentence
- Twenty seconds maximum before the title card

If average view percentage is under 30%, rewrite cold opens for three episodes in
a row before changing anything else. It is almost always the opening.

## Description and chapters

Generated automatically, in this order: two sentences restating the argument,
chapters, sources, AI disclosure. Chapters matter more than people think — they
create seek points, and seeking correlates with longer sessions. Sources matter
because this audience checks, and a visible source list converts a sceptical
viewer into a subscriber.

## The outlier rule

Measured against **your own median**, not against absolute view counts. An
episode at 2.5x your median is the channel telling you something. The weekly
reach report flags these.

When you get one: make three more like it within the month. Not the same company
— the same *shape*. Was it the industry? The era? The type of failure? The fact
that it was a company people personally used? Work out which variable carried it
and repeat that variable.

This is the single highest-return action on YouTube and most people skip it
because they're attached to the content plan they made in week one.

## Underperformers

Under 40% of median, and the video is at least three weeks old:

1. **New thumbnail first.** Cheapest change, biggest effect. Wait two weeks.
2. **Then the title**, if the thumbnail didn't move it. Wait two more weeks.
3. Never change both at once — you'll learn nothing about which one was wrong.

Don't delete or unlist a flop. It costs you nothing to leave up and it still
contributes watch hours toward the threshold.

## Publishing rhythm

Tuesdays and Fridays, 17:30 IST. That lands in the US morning and the Indian
evening, which is the overlap you want for an English business audience. One
Short per day.

Consistency matters more than the specific slot. Pick it, keep it, and let the
scheduler handle it so a bad week at your day job doesn't break the streak.
